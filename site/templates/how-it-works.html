How it works

From project assumptions to a usable cash-flow forecast

CashflowPot starts with the project information you already know: contract value, duration, activities, timing and commercial terms. It turns those inputs into a focused construction cash-flow forecast you can review, adjust and communicate.

You do not need to maintain a fully cost-loaded Primavera P6 or MS Project programme just to answer a cash-flow question.

The workflow

  1. Define the project and commercial assumptions
  2. Build the activity forecast and timing
  3. Calculate inflow, outflow and cash position
  4. Review and adjust scenarios
  5. Export and communicate the forecast

Step 1

Define the project

Start with the project and contract assumptions that control how value turns into cash.

  • Contract value and project duration
  • Advance payment and its recovery assumptions
  • Retention and retention-release timing
  • Payment period between valuation and receipt
  • Defects-liability period and end-of-project release assumptions
  • WIEB — the share of completed work carried into the following billing period

Step 2

Build the activity forecast

Break the project into activities at the level needed for a credible forecast rather than a detailed construction schedule.

  • Activity start and finish timing
  • Activity value and cost assumptions
  • The subcontracted portion where relevant
  • Timing that drives earned value and the expected pattern of project outflow
  • Enough structure to explain the cash profile without unnecessary scheduling detail

Step 3

CashflowPot calculates the forecast

The calculation engine applies the reviewed activity and commercial assumptions consistently across the project timeline.

  • Earned-value progression from the activity forecast
  • Expected billing and cash-in timing
  • Direct and subcontract-related cash-out timing
  • Advance, retention and payment-delay effects
  • Monthly inflow, outflow, net cash and cumulative cash position
  • The peak negative cash position and associated funding requirement

For the detailed terminology, timing conventions and formulas, use the Methodology page.

Step 4

Review and adjust scenarios

A forecast becomes useful when you can test the assumptions behind it rather than treat the first result as fixed.

Activity timing Payment terms Retention Advance payment Subcontracting Project duration

Keep independent scenarios for different decisions—for example a tender forecast, base forecast, current forecast or recovery scenario—and compare how the cash requirement changes.

Step 5

Export and communicate

The forecast is intended to become a working project deliverable, not just a chart on screen.

  • Tender cash-flow submissions and commercial review
  • Feasibility studies and project investment review
  • Annual and multi-year budget planning
  • Execution-phase project and management reporting
  • Lender, investor and working-capital discussions
  • Professional Excel schedules that can be reviewed and continued outside the app

Near-term direction

AI-assisted project setup

AI-assisted project setup is in development. The planned workflow lets you describe the project, scope, value and duration and receive a proposed project structure, activities, timing and commercial assumptions as a starting point.

You review those proposed inputs before calculation. AI helps prepare the project definition; the CashflowPot calculation engine remains responsible for the forecast.

Lightweight by design

Use the level of detail the cash-flow decision needs

CashflowPot does not replace Primavera P6, MS Project, ERP, accounting or detailed cost-control systems. Those systems solve broader project-management problems.

CashflowPot is useful when you need a credible cash-flow view quickly—during tendering, feasibility, budgeting, execution reforecasting or financing analysis—without first rebuilding every detail of the project programme.
