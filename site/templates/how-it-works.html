How it works

From the project information you know to the cash decision you need.

CashflowPot separates execution, direct scheduled expenditure, commercial cash timing and financing. Build enough of the model to support the decision, then review how Project Cashflow, Facilities and Cash Position interact.

Fast setup

New scenarios start with the Independent contract curve using a balanced S-curve. You can create a useful first forecast without configuring every advanced setting.

Step 1

Define the project

Enter the contract value and the project information already available. Record known contract terms separately from the assumptions needed to forecast future behaviour.

  • Contract value
  • Advance payment and recovery terms
  • Retention and release terms
  • Payment period
  • Defects Liability Period (DLP)
  • Forecasting assumptions such as WIEB where required

Step 2

Set the inflow forecast method

Independent contract curve — default

Distribute contract value independently over the Work activity execution duration. Choose Linear or S-curve; an S-curve can be Back-loaded, Balanced or Front-loaded.

Activity-linked inflow — alternative

Use the combined Work activity execution profile as the contract-value timing shape. Scheduled Cost is not included in that Work profile.

Step 3

Build Work activities and Scheduled Cost

Model physical execution separately from costs that should simply occur in selected periods.

Work activities

Use S-curve, Linear or Manual Work distributions. Work activities can include direct/subcontracted share, subcontract terms, pre-work timing, WIEB, retention and payment periods.

Scheduled Cost

Enter discrete project expenditure directly by project period. Scheduled Cost creates project outflow but no physical work, client PPC or additional Work duration.

Step 4

Apply contract terms and assumptions

Turn contract-value work and Work activity cost profiles into expected receipts and payments.

  • Advance and advance recovery
  • Work in Excess of Billings (WIEB)
  • Retention
  • Payment period
  • Defects Liability Period (DLP)
  • Subcontract payment, retention and WIEB assumptions

Step 5

Add financing facilities

Model the financing that sits on top of project cashflow, including working capital where required.

  • Scheduled draws for LC, working capital or equipment facilities
  • PPC-linked draws such as PPC Discount
  • Automatic-shortfall draws such as overdraft
  • Cashflow sweep, PPC-linked, scheduled or installment repayment
  • Revolving or non-revolving limits
  • Interest, fees and outstanding principal

Step 6

Review Project Cashflow, Facilities and Cash Position

CashflowPot separates the project's own economics from the financing used to support it.

  • Project Cashflow — Client PPC receipts and Work + Scheduled Cost
  • Facilities — Draw, Repayment, Interest & fees, Outstanding balance
  • Cash Position — Total inflow, Total outflow, Net cash flow, Closing cash balance
  • Peak Funding Requirement
  • Peak Facility Usage and Outstanding Facilities
  • Cash after Debt Settlement

Step 7

Revise scenarios and export

Keep separate tender, base, current or recovery scenarios and update them as the project changes. Export a professional Excel schedule with activities, facilities, KPIs, charts and reconciliation when the forecast needs to move into a management, tender or financing workflow.

A detailed programme can inform activity timing when it is available and current. CashflowPot does not require a detailed programme for every cash decision: a focused execution model can be more appropriate for tendering, lender review or a rapid mid-project reforecast.

AI-assisted project setup

AI-assisted project setup is in development. AI may propose Work activities, Scheduled Cost, costs, durations, subcontracted shares, financing facilities and starting assumptions from a short project description, but the user reviews those inputs and the CashflowPot calculation engine remains responsible for the forecast.
