Execution and cost

Separate physical work from costs that simply need to be paid.

Work activities

Model physical, cost-loaded execution using an S-curve, Linear distribution or Manual Work schedule. Work activities determine project Work duration and can carry subcontract payment terms.

Scheduled Cost

Place equipment, materials or other discrete project expenditure directly into chosen project periods. Scheduled Cost creates project outflow and funding need without creating physical work, client PPC or extra Work duration.

Two ways to model contract inflow

Choose the inflow method that fits the decision.

Independent contract curve

Default and recommended for new scenarios. Contract value follows its own linear or balanced S-curve over the Work activity execution duration. You can make the S-curve Back-loaded, Balanced or Front-loaded, then apply client contract terms and forecasting assumptions.

Activity-linked inflow

Use the combined Work activity execution profile as the shape for contract value when expected client-side value should follow the Work pattern. Scheduled Cost is excluded from the Work denominator and does not create client value.

Financing facilities

Test how the project is actually funded.

Place financing on top of the project cashflow instead of hiding it inside one interest assumption.

  • Overdraft and automatic-shortfall funding
  • Letter of Credit and scheduled draws
  • PPC Discount and PPC-linked draws
  • Working capital and equipment facilities
  • Cashflow sweep, PPC-linked, scheduled or installment repayment
  • Revolving and non-revolving limits
  • Interest, fees and outstanding facility principal

Built for decisions, not one workflow

Use the forecast when the financial question appears.

Tender & bid/no-bid

Estimate funding exposure before a detailed resource-loaded tender programme exists, and compare several opportunities when deciding what the business can finance.

Contractor financing

Prepare a project funding view for banks and financing discussions, then test how payment period, retention, advance recovery, Scheduled Cost and facility terms affect the requirement.

Lender review

Recreate a contractor cash-flow view with explicit contract terms, assumptions and financing facilities, then challenge the timing and adequacy of the proposed funding.

Mid-project reforecast

Reforecast the remaining cash position when the programme, procurement timing or financing structure has changed and historic loading no longer represents the current commercial reality.

Execution and cash are different layers

A programme can inform the forecast without becoming the forecast.

Planning software can provide detailed programme information and time-phased work or cost. CashflowPot focuses on the commercial and financing layers that follow: how execution, project costs, contract terms and forecasting assumptions translate into billings, receipts, payments and funding requirements.

When detailed planning data is current, it can inform activity timing. When it is unavailable, disproportionate to the decision, or stale, CashflowPot can use a focused mathematical execution profile instead.

Contract terms and assumptions

Model the conditions that move work into cash.

  • Advance payment and recovery
  • Retention and release timing
  • Payment period
  • Work in Excess of Billings (WIEB)
  • Defects Liability Period (DLP)
  • Subcontracted share and subcontract commercial terms
  • Execution curve and timing assumptions
